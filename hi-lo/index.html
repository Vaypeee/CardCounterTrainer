The Hi-Lo card counting system

Hi-Lo is the most widely used blackjack count: simple enough to keep at casino speed, strong enough for serious play. This page covers the card values, the running and true count, how to size your bets, and the index plays that matter most.

1Hi-Lo card values

Every card gets a tag of +1, 0 or −1. Low cards that leave the shoe are good for the player, high cards are bad:

Hi-Lo is a level 1 count (tags are only ±1) and it is balanced: one deck holds twenty +1 cards and twenty −1 cards, so a complete shoe always counts back to 0. The system was introduced by Harvey Dubner in 1963 and popularised by Stanford Wong’s Professional Blackjack.

2Keeping the running count

Start at 0 after every shuffle and add the tag of each card you see, including other players’ cards and the dealer’s hole card when it is turned over.

Round 1: 5 K 3 | Q 9 | dealer 6  →  +1 −1 +1 −1 0 +1 = +1
Round 2: 2 4 | 8 A | dealer 5  →  +1 +1 0 −1 +1 = +2, running count +3
  • Count in pairs: a 10 and a 5 cancel out, so skip them and only add what is left.
  • Learn to recognise common groups at a glance (two tens and a small card = −1).
  • Practise until you can count a full deck in under 25 seconds without mistakes: that is the Speed Count drill.

3Converting to the true count

In a multi-deck game the running count must be divided by the number of decks still to be played:

True count = running count ÷ decks remaining
Running count +9, about 3 decks left → true count +3

Estimate the decks left from the discard tray to the nearest half deck and round the result down (floor). Most published Hi-Lo indices assume floored true counts. A step-by-step explanation with a calculator is in the beginner’s guide.

4Betting with Hi-Lo

Each +1 of true count adds roughly 0.5% to the player’s edge. A typical 6-deck game starts at about −0.5%, so the edge turns positive around TC +1 to +2. Raise your bet only from there:

6How strong is Hi-Lo?

Counting systems are compared on three measures: betting correlation (how well the count predicts your edge, used to size bets), playing efficiency (how well it guides strategy changes) and insurance correlation. Hi-Lo scores about 0.97 / 0.51 / 0.76. That is very strong for betting and adequate for playing. Level 2 counts gain a little playing efficiency at the cost of much harder counting.
