The Illustrious 18 and Fab 4

Card counters do not play basic strategy blindly. In a few situations the true count says the opposite play is better. Don Schlesinger showed in Blackjack Attack that a short list of these “index plays” captures most of the value of playing with the count. Here they are for Hi-Lo, and how to make them automatic.

1How an index works

Each play has an index, a true count. At or above the index, you make the deviation; below it, you play basic strategy. Example: 16 against a dealer 10 has an index of 0. Basic strategy says hit (or surrender), but as soon as the true count is 0 or higher, stand: the shoe holds enough tens that hitting busts too often.

Negative indices work the same way. 13 vs 2 has an index of −1: stand at −1 and above, hit only when the count drops below −1.

2Number one: insurance

The most valuable index of all is insurance at true count +3 or more. Insurance pays 2:1 and wins when the dealer’s hole card is a ten. Normally fewer than a third of the cards are tens, so it is a bad bet. At TC +3 the proportion rises above one third and the bet turns profitable.

5What matters most

  • Insurance at +3 by a wide margin.
  • 16 vs 10 and 15 vs 10: they come up constantly.
  • 12 vs 2 and 12 vs 3, then the doubling plays (10 vs 10, 11 vs A, 9 vs 2).
  • The negative indices (13 vs 2 and 3, 12 vs 4–6) matter less for your edge, since you bet small at negative counts, but they are easy once you know the others.

Learn basic strategy perfectly first (full chart). A deviation is only worth something if your basic play is already automatic.
