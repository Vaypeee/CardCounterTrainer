Is card counting illegal?

Short answer: counting cards in your head is not a crime in the United States, the United Kingdom and most other countries. But casinos are private businesses, and they do not have to let you play. Here is what that means in practice.

1What the law says

Card counting is simply paying attention to the cards that have been played and using that information. No law forbids using your memory at a blackjack table, and courts have treated it as legitimate play, not cheating. What is illegal almost everywhere is using a device to help you, or colluding with the dealer.

2What casinos can do

  • Back-off: a floor supervisor tells you that you may keep playing other games but not blackjack.
  • Flat-betting: you may stay, but only if you stop varying your bets.
  • Early shuffles: the dealer shuffles as soon as you raise your bet, which removes your edge.
  • Trespass: in many places a casino can ask you to leave and ban you. Coming back after a formal trespass notice can be an offence.
  • Information sharing: casinos share photos of known advantage players with each other.

3Nevada and New Jersey

In Nevada, casinos may refuse service to card counters and bar them from the property. In New Jersey, the state Supreme Court ruled in Uston v. Resorts International (1982) that Atlantic City casinos cannot exclude players for counting. They respond instead with countermeasures: more frequent shuffles, betting limits and continuous shuffling machines. Rules elsewhere vary by country and state; this page is general information, not legal advice.

4Devices are a different story

Using a phone, a computer or any other device to count or to calculate odds at the table is a crime in Nevada (NRS 465.075) and is prohibited in casinos virtually everywhere. Counting must stay in your head. That is exactly the skill a card counting trainer builds.

5What about online blackjack?

Counting does not work in online blackjack driven by a random number generator: the virtual deck is reshuffled for every hand. Live-dealer tables use real shoes but are usually shuffled at about half the shoe, which leaves too little penetration for counting to pay.

6How counters avoid attention

  • Moderate bet spreads and gradual increases rather than jumping from the minimum to the maximum.
  • Short sessions and spreading play across casinos and shifts.
  • Acting like a normal player: chatting, not staring at every card, not hesitating before each bet.
  • All of this only works if the count itself takes no visible effort. That is why counters drill until keeping the true count is effortless.
