Blackjack basic strategy chart

Basic strategy is the best way to play every hand when you do not count: it cuts the house edge to roughly half a percent in a good game. It is also the foundation of card counting, since counters only deviate from it in a few spots. Below is the full chart, then how to learn it.

1Rules this chart assumes

4 to 8 decks, dealer stands on soft 17 (S17), double after split allowed (DAS), late surrender, blackjack pays 3:2. These are the rules used throughout this site. Find your hand on the left and the dealer’s up card at the top.

2Hard totals

A hard hand has no ace, or an ace that must count as 1.

3Soft hands

A soft hand has an ace counted as 11 (A,6 is soft 17). It cannot bust with one more card, which is why you double soft hands so often against weak dealer cards.

4Pairs

Always split aces and eights; never split tens or fives (play 5,5 as a hard 10).

5If your table’s rules differ

  • Dealer hits soft 17 (H17): double 11 vs A, double A,8 vs 6 (else stand), double A,7 vs 2 (else stand), and with surrender also surrender 15 vs A, 17 vs A and 8,8 vs A.
  • No surrender: every “Rh” becomes a hit.
  • No double after split: split 2,2 and 3,3 only vs 4–7, never split 4,4, and split 6,6 only vs 3–6.
  • Blackjack pays 6:5: the strategy barely changes, but the house edge jumps by about 1.4%. Avoid these tables.
  • Insurance: never take it without counting. Counters take it at Hi-Lo true count +3 or more.

6How to learn it quickly

  1. Hard totals first. Most decisions follow one idea: the dealer’s 2–6 are “bust cards” (stand on 12–16 against them, except 12 vs 2 and 3), 7–A are strong (hit until 17).
  2. Then pairs, which have the fewest exceptions: always A,A and 8,8; never 5,5 and T,T; 9,9 splits except against 7, 10 and A.
  3. Soft hands last. The doubling zone widens as the hand improves: A,2–A,3 vs 5–6, A,4–A,5 vs 4–6, A,6–A,7 vs 3–6.
  4. Drill with flashcards until each answer is instant, then play hands without looking at the chart.
